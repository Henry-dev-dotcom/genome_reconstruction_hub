Genome Reconstruction of a Novel Emerging Pathogen

Learn how scientists move from an unknown bacterial pathogen to a reconstructed genome using Illumina short reads, Nanopore long reads, de novo assembly, hybrid assembly, and careful reference comparison.

The Scenario

Understanding the Group 2 Case

The central issue is that normal diagnostic methods could not identify the pathogen. The researchers therefore used whole-genome sequencing and genome assembly to characterize the organism.

Sequencing Data

Platform Reads Read Length
Illumina 20 million 150 bp
Nanopore 250,000 12–20 kb

Main Scientific Challenge

At first, database searches found no closely related reference genome. Because of this, the team had to reconstruct the genome from the sequencing reads themselves using de novo assembly.

A related species genome with 96% similarity was later discovered. This reference can help with comparison and annotation, but it should not replace the de novo assembly because it may introduce reference bias.

Expanded Lessons

Learning Modules

Click each module to read the full explanation.

Whole-genome sequencing, or WGS, is the process of reading the DNA sequence of an organism across its whole genome. For an unknown pathogen, WGS is powerful because it does not depend only on one expected target. It gives researchers broad information about identity, gene content, virulence factors, resistance genes, and evolutionary relationship.

In this case, conventional diagnostics failed because the organism was not easily recognized by routine laboratory methods. WGS allows researchers to move beyond traditional testing and examine the organism at genome level.

Key takeaway: WGS is used because the pathogen is unknown and needs genome-level characterization.

Illumina sequencing produces millions of short reads. In the case, it produced 20 million reads of 150 bp. The major strength of Illumina is high base-level accuracy and high depth of coverage. This makes it useful for polishing assemblies and identifying small sequence differences.

However, Illumina reads are short. When the genome contains repeats, insertion sequences, mobile elements, or complex regions, short reads may not be able to show how distant regions connect. This is one reason Assembly A became fragmented.

Simple picture: Illumina gives many tiny but accurate puzzle pieces.

Nanopore sequencing produces long reads. In the case, the Nanopore reads were 12–20 kb, which is far longer than the 150 bp Illumina reads. These long reads can span repeated regions and connect contigs that short reads cannot join.

Nanopore reads may have higher raw error rates than Illumina reads, but their length makes them extremely useful for improving assembly continuity. When combined with Illumina data, the long reads provide structure while the short reads help correct errors.

Simple picture: Nanopore gives larger puzzle pieces that show how sections connect.

De novo assembly means reconstructing a genome without using a reference genome. This is the correct starting point when the organism is novel and no close reference is available. The assembler looks for overlaps between reads and uses those overlaps to build longer sequences called contigs.

De novo assembly is useful because it allows the new organism to reveal its own genome structure. It can detect unique genes, insertions, and genomic regions that may not exist in a known reference. Its limitation is that it can become fragmented, especially when only short reads are available.

Key takeaway: De novo assembly builds the genome from scratch.

A contig is a continuous DNA sequence produced by assembly. A high number of contigs usually means the genome is fragmented. N50 is a measure of assembly continuity. A higher N50 means that the assembled sequences are longer and more connected. Coverage describes how much of the genome is represented by the sequencing data or assembly.

Assembly A had 375 contigs, N50 of 40 kb, and 95% coverage. This suggests an incomplete and fragmented assembly. Assembly B had 15 contigs, N50 of 2.3 Mb, and 99.7% coverage, showing major improvement in continuity and completeness.

Best rule: fewer contigs + higher N50 + higher coverage = better assembly.

Hybrid assembly combines short-read and long-read sequencing data. In this case, Illumina reads provide accuracy, while Nanopore reads provide long-range connections. The result is a much better assembly than using Illumina alone.

This explains the improvement from Assembly A to Assembly B. The number of contigs dropped from 375 to 15, N50 increased from 40 kb to 2.3 Mb, and coverage improved from 95% to 99.7%.

Key takeaway: Hybrid assembly uses the strength of both sequencing platforms.

Reference mapping means aligning sequencing reads to an existing genome. It can be useful when a closely related reference exists because it helps with comparison and variant detection. However, it can be risky for a novel pathogen.

Reference bias happens when the new genome is forced to fit the reference genome too closely. This may hide genes, insertions, rearrangements, or unique regions that are present in the new pathogen but absent from the reference.

Use the 96% similar genome for comparison, not as the primary reconstruction method.

Genome reconstruction is important because it can help identify the pathogen, discover virulence genes, detect antimicrobial resistance genes, support diagnostic test development, and guide surveillance. For an emerging infection, genome data can help public health teams understand risk and respond more effectively.

In molecular medicine, this connects laboratory genomics with patient care, epidemiology, diagnosis, treatment planning, and outbreak response.

Genome reconstruction turns unknown infection into actionable biological information.

Assembly Metrics

Assembly A vs Assembly B

The case becomes easy when you understand that Assembly B is more complete because long reads improved continuity.

Metric Assembly A Assembly B Interpretation
Contigs 375 15 Assembly B is much less fragmented.
N50 40 kb 2.3 Mb Assembly B has longer continuous sequences.
Coverage 95% 99.7% Assembly B represents almost the entire genome.

Why Assembly A is Weak

It is fragmented, has many contigs, has low N50, and does not fully cover the genome. It probably struggled because short reads could not connect repeated or complex regions.

Why Assembly B is Stronger

Nanopore long reads helped bridge gaps and join contigs. This increased continuity and made the genome reconstruction more complete.

Procedure

Genome Reconstruction Workflow

This is the practical procedure researchers would follow in the case scenario.

01

Collect and Culture the Isolate

Obtain the bacterial isolate from patients with severe febrile illness.

02

Extract High-Quality DNA

Extract DNA carefully, especially because long intact DNA improves Nanopore sequencing.

03

Generate Reads

Sequence with Illumina for accurate short reads and Nanopore for long reads.

04

Perform Quality Control

Remove poor-quality reads, adapters, and possible contaminants.

05

Run De Novo Assembly

Build the first genome assembly without a reference because the pathogen is novel.

06

Improve with Hybrid Assembly

Combine short and long reads to reduce contigs and increase N50 and coverage.

07

Assess Assembly Quality

Check contigs, N50, coverage, completeness, contamination, and possible errors.

08

Annotate and Interpret

Identify genes, virulence factors, resistance markers, and taxonomic relationships.

09

Compare with Reference

Use the 96% similar genome carefully for comparison and validation.

Presentation Practice

Short-Answer Questions

Use these to practise explaining the topic in your own words.

1. Why is de novo assembly important in this case?

Because the pathogen is novel and no close reference genome was available at first, the genome had to be reconstructed from the sequencing reads themselves.

2. Why did Assembly A become fragmented?

Assembly A likely relied heavily on short reads, which may fail to span repetitive or complex genomic regions.

3. Why did Nanopore reads improve Assembly B?

Nanopore reads are long enough to bridge gaps, connect contigs, and resolve repeat-rich regions, improving continuity and coverage.

4. What is reference bias?

Reference bias occurs when sequencing data from a new organism is forced to fit an existing reference, causing unique regions to be missed or misinterpreted.

5. What strategy is best?

Hybrid de novo assembly is best, followed by polishing, quality assessment, annotation, and careful comparison with the 96% similar reference genome.

6. Why does this matter clinically?

Genome reconstruction helps identify the pathogen, understand its genes, support diagnosis, guide surveillance, and inform public health response.

Case Challenge

Apply What You Have Learned

This section helps your group move from memorising definitions to defending your recommended genome reconstruction strategy.

Scenario

Your team has Assembly A from Illumina reads only. It has 375 contigs, an N50 of 40 kb, and 95% coverage. Nanopore long reads are then added and Assembly B improves to 15 contigs, an N50 of 2.3 Mb, and 99.7% coverage. Later, a related species genome with 96% similarity is discovered.

Your Decision

Recommend hybrid de novo assembly as the primary strategy because the pathogen is novel and long reads clearly improved assembly quality.

How to Use the Reference

Use the 96% similar genome for comparison, annotation support, and validation, but avoid using it as the only guide because unique pathogen regions may be missed.

How to Defend It

State that Assembly B has fewer contigs, higher N50, and higher coverage, meaning it is more complete and more reliable for downstream interpretation.

Academic Support

Reference Notes for Your Group Note

Use these short reference notes when writing your group note or defending your presentation.

Whole-genome sequencing

WGS is useful in outbreak detection because it gives a more detailed DNA fingerprint than older typing methods and can help identify related cases more precisely.

Illumina sequencing

Illumina sequencing by synthesis detects bases as they are incorporated into growing DNA strands in a massively parallel system. It is useful for accurate short-read data.

Nanopore sequencing

Nanopore sequencing directly reads DNA or RNA by detecting current changes as molecules pass through nanopores. Its long reads are valuable for difficult and repetitive genome regions.

Hybrid assembly

Hybrid assembly combines accurate short reads with long reads that resolve genome structure. This explains the improvement from Assembly A to Assembly B in the case scenario.

Assembly assessment

Assembly quality should be checked using metrics such as number of contigs, N50, coverage, completeness, and possible contamination or misassembly.

Annotation and classification

After assembly, the genome should be annotated to identify genes and classified to understand its relationship to known bacteria.
